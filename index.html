Three decisions that shape everything

FC expiry is the trigger

Outreach is anchored to Fitness Certificate expiry, not SIM/certificate expiry. This is the source of first-mover advantage — so FC-data enrichment is the first domino, not messaging.

Registration is on the critical path

Company → GST → DLT is a hard chain (~2–4 weeks). Legal bulk SMS cannot fire until it clears. Start registration & DLT prep now, in parallel.

Decouple renewals from software

Renewal outreach + payment collection can go live on the FC calendar by Sep 15 even if the polished app slips to late Sep / early Oct. Keep them separate.

Deep dive — how FC-expiry data is actually procured

Two different data needs, two very different answers. Get this right and the government-portal detour becomes optional.

Route A — Government direct (portal registration)LOW feasibility

The old bulk-data route (₹3 crore/yr for VAHAN/Sarathi) was scrapped in 2020. The 2025 replacement shares bulk data only case-by-case, DPDP-compliant, with a CERT-IN security-audit certificate — effectively for government agencies and a narrow approved set. Do not gate September on it. The one angle worth a parallel, GP-owned track: because your partner is a Govt Partner, GP-led access to Karnataka state transport data may be legitimately available.

Route B — Third-party RC-verification APIsPRIMARY

Providers (Surepass, Signzy, Gridlines, Deepvue, IDfy) return, per registration number, the Fitness Certificate validity plus insurance / PUC / MV-tax / permit — and support bulk verification. Input is the RC number, so this enriches vehicles you already track. Pipeline: GP's RC list → batch through the API → FC expiry → cohort calendar. Cost is a few rupees per lookup at volume MED — get quotes.

Route C — GP / dealer-sourced dataFASTEST FIRST STEP

GP's device DB already carries vehicle number, registered mobile, install/expiry date and RTO zone. Add a one-time Route-B enrichment for FC expiry and your cohort list exists without touching any government portal.

Recommendation: Primary = Route C + Route B for the existing base. Route A stays a parallel, GP-led, net-new track with no September dependency.

Deep dive — messaging: bypass assessed, compliant path wins

You asked whether tools exist to send without compliance. They're advertised — here's what they actually are, and why the compliant route is faster and cheaper for a renewal base.

Channel"Bypass" option & mechanismPriceWhy it fails here
SMS Grey/"Quick SMS" via international (ILDO) routes — Fast2SMS, SMSLocal, SpeedSMS; random numeric sender ID ~₹5 / SMS Illegal under TCCCPR 2018; carrier firewalls block; penalties up to ₹1L/instance; 20–40× cost
SMS GSM/SIM-farm gateway (e.g. Teleos) — sends from your own SIM via an Android app App/subscription Tiny throughput; number flagged/banned; unusable at 1.2L scale
WhatsApp Unofficial bulk sender — WhatsApp-Web automation / "unofficial API" panels; no template approval ₹1k–5k/mo tools Violates Meta ToS; numbers banned within a few thousand sends — fatal mid-campaign
SMS Compliant: DLT (PE + header + template) + gateway; service/transactional route ~₹0.12–0.25 / SMS Reaches DND for existing customers; DLT live in 1–7 days; ~₹500–5,900 one-time
WhatsApp Compliant: Official Business API via BSP (AiSensy / Interakt / Gupshup / Wati) ~₹0.7–1.1 / msg + plan Stable at scale; rich templates + payment button; template approval in days
Bottom line: there is no reliable non-compliant route for a 120K renewal push. For an existing customer base the compliant, service-routed path is both legal and higher-deliverability — it's the faster lane, not the obstacle.
